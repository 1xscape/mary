4.1

Before reading

“Your feed is not your personality.” How much do you agree?

Your feed is not your personality

Your social-media feed can reveal a lot about your interests, but it rarely shows the full picture of who you are. Most people choose what they post. They share some moments, ignore others and usually present a version of life that feels worth showing.

Some people post regularly and enjoy talking to an audience. Others hardly ever upload anything, even though they scroll every day. A quiet account does not necessarily belong to a quiet person. Someone may be confident and funny offline but prefer to keep their personal life private online.

There is also a difference between long-term interests and what is happening right now. Your feed might show that you love fashion in general, while your recent activity shows that you are currently obsessed with one particular show, creator or trend.

In other words, a feed is a collection of choices, not a complete personality test. It can tell us what someone tends to watch, save and follow, but it cannot show every side of that person.

HW C

New reading: The silent scroller

Some people are online all the time but leave almost no public trace. They watch Stories, save posts, send videos to friends and follow new accounts, yet they hardly ever upload anything themselves. Researchers and social-media managers sometimes call these users “lurkers”, although the word can sound more negative than the behaviour really is.

For many people, scrolling is entertainment rather than self-expression. They may know every current meme and follow dozens of creators without wanting an audience of their own. Others simply prefer private conversations: they send content to a few close friends instead of posting it publicly. Their account may look inactive, while their actual online routine is surprisingly busy.
